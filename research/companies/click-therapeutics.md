# Click Therapeutics

**Researched:** August 7, 2026

## Company Overview

- **Location**: New York, NY (80 White Street).
- **Founded**: 2012, by **David Benshoof Klein** (CEO) and **Wei "Kiddy" Zhou**. Klein spent 20+ years in life sciences before this, including as Managing Director at Opus Point Partners, which stood up companies like Cougar Biotechnology (acquired by J&J) and Chelsea Therapeutics (acquired by Lundbeck).
- **Stage**: Late-stage private company. Raised a **$50M Series D** (reported May 2026), part of **~$200-270M total raised** (source figures vary — Crunchbase/Clay report different totals, not independently reconciled here).
- **Core problem**: most drug and behavioral treatments for brain- and body-based conditions (depression, migraine, chronic disease) rely on a patient doing something consistently outside the clinic — taking a pill correctly, practicing a cognitive exercise, sticking to a regimen. The Click bet: a clinically validated, FDA-regulated **mobile app** can be prescribed like a drug to close that gap, either alone or stacked on top of a drug.
- **In one plain sentence**: Click Therapeutics does not make pills or devices — it makes prescription-grade *software*, validated in clinical trials the same way a drug would be, that a doctor prescribes to a patient phone.

## Technology & Products

Click platform develops **prescription digital therapeutics (PDTs)** — regulated mobile apps that go through FDA review and clinical trials like a drug, rather than shipping as a general wellness app.

- **Analogy**: think of it like a **classifier or intervention policy trained and validated the same way a drug efficacy is** — instead of a molecule interacting with a receptor, the active ingredient is a sequence of app-delivered cognitive/behavioral exercises, and the FDA reviews the clinical trial data on that sequence exactly like it would a drug Phase 2/3 data.
- **CT-132** — the company own first-in-class PDT for **preventive treatment of episodic migraine**, granted **FDA De Novo marketing authorization in 2025** (the first PDT authorized for migraine prevention in the US), after also receiving FDA Breakthrough Device Designation. Rollout continues past authorization into distribution/engagement partnerships — for example a **January 2026 partnership with Ultrahuman** (smart ring maker) launching **"Migraine PowerPlug,"** pairing wearable biometric data with the CT-132 digital therapy.[^2026-08-11]
- **Rejoyn** — developed with **Otsuka**, an FDA-authorized PDT for **adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD)** symptoms — notable as one of the first PDTs cleared for a psychiatric indication.
- **Schizophrenia program (CT-155)** — a PDT candidate targeting **negative symptoms of schizophrenia**, now past pivotal evaluation: the Phase III **CONVOKE** trial hit its primary endpoint (significant reduction in negative symptoms), and it carries **FDA Breakthrough Device Designation**. This looks like the next FDA-authorization/launch target after CT-132 and Rejoyn.[^2026-08-11]
- **Click SE ("Software-Enhanced Drug")** — a newer offering (launched 2024, responding to FDA guidance on prescription drug use-related software/PDURS) that pairs software directly with a drug label, rather than standing alone — for example an app bundled with a prescription to improve adherence or outcomes. This is the piece closest to a "helps the drug work better" model; CT-132/Rejoyn/CT-155 are the other model, where the app itself is the standalone active ingredient, closer to digitally-delivered CBT or skills-training than passive symptom monitoring. Click runs both models side by side rather than picking one.[^2026-08-11]
- **Pipeline expansion**: beyond the above, Click has a **collaboration with Indivior** to co-develop and commercialize a PDT for **substance use disorders** (a new indication area for the company), plus earlier-stage named programs **Clickadian** (insomnia) and **Clickheart** (acute coronary syndrome), and earliest-stage clinical study of PDTs for overactive bladder, chronic lower back pain, and obesity.[^2026-08-11]
- Click has also acquired assets from two now-defunct DTx competitors, **Pear Therapeutics** (2023 bankruptcy) and **Better Therapeutics**, consolidating IP/pipeline from a shrinking field.
- Full pipeline: [clicktherapeutics.com/products](https://www.clicktherapeutics.com/products)

## Market & Competition

- Sells primarily to **pharma partners** (Otsuka, and past ties to Sanofi Ventures, Boehringer Ingelheim) who co-develop and often help commercialize a PDT, and to **payers/health systems** who reimburse a prescription app like a drug — not a direct-to-consumer app store play.
- **Differentiator**: Click has survived and grown while the digital therapeutics sector consolidated hard — both Pear Therapeutics and Better Therapeutics went bankrupt, and Click picked up assets from both.
- **Why the market is hard**: reimbursement is the central bottleneck — insurers and CMS do not have a mature, standard billing code/pathway for prescribing an app the way they do for a drug. This financial fragility is exactly what killed Pear Therapeutics despite having FDA clearances.
- Other players: **Akili Interactive**, **Omada Health**, **Big Health**, **Voluntis**, **Biofourmis** — a fairly small, still-shaking-out competitive set rather than a crowded market.

- What stands in for a revenue/traction signal instead: in **April 2026**, the Boehringer Ingelheim $50M Series D came with **dedicated commercial funding earmarked to help bring CT-155 to market** — a pharma partner funding commercialization (not just R&D) implies its own diligence found the asset commercially credible. If real prescription/revenue numbers ever surface, they are more likely to show up in Otsuka or Boehringer investor filings than in a Click disclosure, since Click itself is private with no earnings reports.[^2026-08-11]
