# Johnson & Johnson (NYSE: JNJ)

*Last researched: 2026-07-29. Reformatted 2026-08-04 to the updated skill template (bullets-and-bold throughout, CS/math-mapped analogies); facts spot-checked against recent news — no material changes found, see footnote.*[^2026-08-04]

## 1. Company Overview

J&J is a ~140-year-old New Jersey biopharma + medical-technology company, currently mid-transformation through two major corporate separations.

- **Headquarters:** New Brunswick, New Jersey — the world headquarters campus sits directly next to Rutgers University's main campus; the two institutions have deep historical ties in the New Brunswick community.
- **Founding story:** founded in **1886** by three brothers — **Robert Wood Johnson I, James Wood Johnson, and Edward Mead Johnson**.
  - Robert Wood Johnson I heard a speech by antiseptic-surgery pioneer Joseph Lister in 1885 and was inspired to commercialize sterile, ready-to-use surgical dressings — at the time most bandages were prepared crudely at the bedside, and infection was a leading cause of surgical death.
  - Started with **14 employees** (8 women, 6 men); formally incorporated in **1887**, one year after operations began.
  - Robert Wood Johnson I served as first president.
- **Current CEO: Joaquín Duato** — Spanish-American executive, born 1962 in Valencia, Spain (dual Spain/US citizen).
  - MBA from **ESADE Business School** (Barcelona); Master of International Management from **Thunderbird School of Global Management** (Arizona).
  - Joined J&J in **1989** through Janssen Pharmaceuticals in Spain, rose through the ranks over three decades.
  - Named CEO **August 2021** (effective January 2022) — the company's 8th CEO since going public in 1944. Also Chairman of the Board.
- **Corporate structure:** historically three segments (Pharmaceutical, MedTech, Consumer Health). In **2023** J&J **spun off its Consumer Health division as Kenvue** (Tylenol, Band-Aid, Listerine, Neutrogena) via IPO and split-off — one of the largest corporate spinoffs in history. J&J is now a pure-play biopharma + medtech company with two segments:
  - **Innovative Medicine** (pharmaceuticals) — ~48% of 2025 sales
  - **MedTech** (devices) — ~51–52% of 2025 sales
  - **A further major structural change is underway:** on **October 14, 2025**, J&J announced it will **separate its Orthopaedics business (DePuy Synthes)** into a standalone public company within 18–24 months, most likely via a tax-free spinoff. Orthopaedics is ~30% of the MedTech segment (~$9.2B in FY2024 sales) and has been growing more slowly than the rest of the portfolio. **Namal Nawana** was named Worldwide President of DePuy Synthes to lead it through separation and continue running it afterward. J&J has said it doesn't expect further material updates on the transaction until mid-2026; as of this reformatting pass (early August 2026) no material update had been disclosed beyond the October 2025 announcement.
- **Scale:**
  - 2025 full-year revenue: **$94.2 billion** (6.0% reported growth, 5.3% operational growth)
  - Innovative Medicine: $28.8B–$45.7B depending on source cut (segment reporting is inconsistent across secondary sources — verify against the 10-K directly); MedTech: **~$30.4B**
  - Employees: **~138,200** (2025)
  - Operates in **~60 countries**; sells products in **175+ countries**; **~250 subsidiaries**
  - Market capitalization: **~$599 billion** as of July 2026 (up from ~$560B+ in early 2026) — one of the largest healthcare companies in the world by market cap
  - 2025 adjusted diluted EPS: **$10.58** (up 9.7% from $9.64 in 2024); reported EPS $11.03
  - **Q2 2026 update:** revenue **$25.3 billion** (up 6.6% YoY), beating consensus; Innovative Medicine sales +7.8% to $16.38B, MedTech +4.5% to $8.93B. J&J raised full-year 2026 guidance to **$99.9B–$100.6B** in sales (operational growth 6.5–7.1%) and adjusted operational EPS to $11.50–$11.65 — putting the company on track to cross **$100 billion in annual revenue for the first time in its 140-year history**.[^2026-07-29b]
- **Mission (as stated by the company):** profoundly change the trajectory of health for humanity, leveraging its dual position in pharma and medtech to address disease across the full spectrum from prevention/diagnosis through treatment.

## 2. Technology & Products / Pipeline

Think of J&J's pipeline as two parallel "product portfolios" running side by side — a drug portfolio (Innovative Medicine) and a hardware portfolio (MedTech) — each with its own competitive dynamics.

### Innovative Medicine (pharma) segment

- **Key therapeutic areas:** Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience, Pulmonary Hypertension, Infectious Diseases, and Cardiovascular & Metabolism.
- **Major marketed drugs:**
  - **Darzalex (daratumumab)** — a monoclonal antibody for multiple myeloma (blood cancer); one of J&J's largest and fastest-growing drugs.
  - **Stelara (ustekinumab)** — historically J&J's top immunology drug (psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's, ulcerative colitis), now in steep, well-documented decline: sales fell ~8.1% (operational) in Q1 2025 and an accelerating **42.7% in Q2 2025** as 8+ biosimilars (Amgen's Wezlana, Sandoz's Pyzchiva, Biocon's Yesintek, and others) launched at 46–90% discounts, and PBMs (CVS, Optum) began excluding or de-prioritizing it on formularies. Stelara was ~18% of Innovative Medicine sales in 2024 (peak ~$11B in 2023); analysts project it falling to roughly $2.7B by 2027. **The textbook "patent cliff" case study for the segment.**
  - **Tremfya (guselkumab)** — J&J's newer IL-23 inhibitor, positioned as Stelara's successor. Recently expanded: FDA approved a **2026 label update** making it the only IL-23 inhibitor shown to inhibit structural joint damage progression in psoriatic arthritis, plus approval for Crohn's disease with both subcutaneous and IV induction options — a differentiator versus competitors.
  - **Erleada (apalutamide)** — androgen receptor inhibitor for prostate cancer.
  - **Rybrevant (amivantamab)** — a bispecific antibody for EGFR-mutated non-small-cell lung cancer, often combined with Lazcluze (lazertinib). In **December 2025** the FDA approved **Rybrevant FASPRO** (amivantamab + hyaluronidase), the first subcutaneous version — turning what was a lengthy IV infusion into a quick injection, a big quality-of-life upgrade for cancer patients; dosing schedule further simplified in Feb 2026.
  - **Carvykti (ciltacabtagene autoleucel)** — a CAR-T cell therapy for relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, developed with Legend Biotech, FDA-approved for earlier treatment lines in **April 2024**. First/only BCMA-targeted CAR-T with this label position.
  - **Spravato (esketamine)** — nasal spray for treatment-resistant depression; a ketamine-derived psychiatric drug administered under medical supervision.
  - **Invega** franchise (paliperidone, various long-acting injectable formulations) — for schizophrenia.
- **Recent M&A / pipeline moves:**
  - **Intra-Cellular Therapies acquisition (2025)** — J&J's largest recent bio-pharma deal, announced Jan 2025 and closed **April 2, 2025**, for **$132/share cash, ~$14.6 billion total equity value**. Brings **CAPLYTA (lumateperone)** — the only FDA-approved drug for bipolar I/II depression as both monotherapy and adjunct, plus schizophrenia approval and a pipeline into generalized anxiety disorder and Alzheimer's-related psychosis/agitation. Expected to add ~$0.7B incremental sales in 2025 (~0.8pp of growth) but dilute adjusted EPS by ~$0.25 in 2025, narrowing to ~$0.21 in 2026.
  - **Ambrx acquisition** — completed March 7, 2024, ~$2.0B all-cash (~$1.8B net of cash), $28.00/share. Brings a synthetic-biology **antibody-drug conjugate (ADC)** platform for next-gen targeted cancer therapies.
  - **Abiomed acquisition** — completed December 22, 2022 (an earlier deal, not 2024/2025 as sometimes assumed), ~$16.6B enterprise value at $380/share. Sits in the MedTech segment (cardiovascular), not Innovative Medicine.
  - **Correction on Cerevel Therapeutics:** this company was **acquired by AbbVie, not J&J** — AbbVie announced the deal Dec 6, 2023 and closed it Aug 1, 2024 for ~$8.7B ($45/share), for schizophrenia/Parkinson's/mood-disorder pipeline assets. If a source claims "J&J acquired Cerevel," that's a mix-up with a competitor's deal.
  - **R&D investment:** **>$14–17B in 2025** depending on measurement window (trailing-twelve-month spend as of mid-2025 was ~$17.0B, up ~14% YoY), roughly 15–16% of sales — supporting a stated pipeline of **200+ compounds** in development. J&J also committed to invest **>$55 billion in U.S. manufacturing/R&D over the next four years**, a 25% increase versus the prior four-year period.

### MedTech segment

- **Surgery — Ethicon:** sutures, staplers, energy devices, wound closure. In 2025 J&J launched a restructuring of the Surgery franchise to exit non-strategic product lines (~$29M in restructuring costs recorded in fiscal Q2 2025).
- **Orthopaedics — DePuy Synthes:** joint replacement (hips, knees), trauma/spine hardware. Slated for spinoff as a standalone company (see above) — currently ~$9.2B/year in sales, would be the largest comprehensive orthopaedics-focused business by market share once independent.
- **Cardiovascular:** **Abiomed's Impella** heart pumps and **Biosense Webster** electrophysiology (cardiac mapping/ablation catheters for treating arrhythmias like atrial fibrillation).
- **Vision:** **Acuvue** contact lenses and **Tecnis** intraocular lenses (IOLs, implanted during cataract surgery).
- **Recent MedTech recall activity (2025–2026), notable and ongoing:**
  - **Automated Impella Controller (AIC):** a cascading series of recalls tied to a significant **cybersecurity vulnerability**, affecting 100,000+ devices — separate recall actions confirmed in July, September, October (x2), and December 2025.
  - **Impella RP / Impella RP with SmartAssist / Impella RP Flex:** Class I recalls over inaccurate differential-pressure sensor readings.
  - **Cerepak detachable coil systems** (Cerenovus business, used in brain aneurysm treatment): recalled for a higher-than-expected failure-to-detach rate, linked to 4 serious injuries and 1 death.
  - This recall cluster is a real quality/reliability signal in the cardiovascular device business, distinct from and in addition to the talc litigation (see Financials).

### Simple explainers of the underlying tech

Framed for someone comfortable with algorithms/systems thinking — plain-language version included for each.

- **CAR-T cell therapy (Carvykti):** doctors draw a patient's own T-cells (immune cells) from blood, genetically re-engineer them to display a receptor (the "CAR," chimeric antigen receptor) that recognizes a specific marker on the cancer cell (here, BCMA, found on myeloma cells), multiply the modified cells, then infuse them back in.
  - **CS analogy:** like fine-tuning an existing agent on a new classification task, then deploying it to autonomously scan a live environment and act whenever it detects a specific feature — except the "agent" is a living cell and "deployment" means reinfusing it into a patient.
  - **Plain-language line:** it's turning a patient's own immune system into a living, targeted drug — powerful but complex and expensive to manufacture per-patient, unlike an off-the-shelf pill.
- **Impella heart pump:** a catheter-based miniature pump, threaded through an artery (often from the leg) up into the heart's left ventricle, that mechanically pushes blood forward to take over some or all of the heart's pumping work during high-risk procedures or acute heart failure.
  - **CS analogy:** like a failover node that takes over a system's throughput temporarily while the primary process is degraded or restarting — the heart doesn't need to be shut down, just relieved of load until it can resume full capacity on its own.
  - **Plain-language line:** a temporary mechanical "assist" for a heart that's struggling to pump enough blood on its own.
- **Bispecific antibodies (Rybrevant):** a normal antibody has two arms that both grab the same target. A bispecific antibody's two arms are engineered to grab two *different* targets simultaneously — e.g. one arm binds a receptor on the tumor cell (EGFR) and drives a signal-blocking effect, while dual pathway engagement helps overcome resistance mechanisms a single-target drug can't address.
  - **CS analogy:** like a function that takes two distinct input parameters and routes them through one combined handler instead of requiring two separate function calls — one molecule doing the coordinated work that used to require two separate drugs.
- **Antibody-drug conjugates (ADC, via the Ambrx platform):** an antibody that "homes in" on a marker specific to cancer cells is chemically linked to a potent toxic payload (a targeted chemotherapy warhead), released mainly once inside the cancer cell.
  - **CS analogy:** like a conditional/guarded function call — the payload (side effect) only executes once a specific condition (the target antigen) is matched, instead of firing unconditionally against every cell the way traditional systemic chemo does.

More on the full pipeline: [J&J Innovative Medicine pipeline](https://www.jnj.com/innovative-medicine/pipeline) and [J&J MedTech](https://www.jnjmedtech.com/).

## 3. Market & Competition

- **Pharma competitors:** Pfizer, Merck, AbbVie, Roche, Novartis, Eli Lilly, Bristol Myers Squibb, Sanofi, GSK — all large-cap diversified pharma companies competing across oncology, immunology, and other therapeutic areas.
- **MedTech competitors:** Medtronic, Stryker, Boston Scientific, Abbott — the other large diversified device makers, each with a different segment emphasis (Medtronic strong in cardiac devices/diabetes, Stryker strong in orthopaedics/surgical, Boston Scientific strong in cardiovascular/endoscopy, Abbott strong in diagnostics/cardiovascular/nutrition).
- **What differentiates J&J:**
  - **Dual pharma + medtech model** — genuinely unusual at this scale; most peers are pure-play biopharma (Pfizer, Merck, AbbVie) or pure-play devices (Medtronic, Stryker). This diversification cushions patent-cliff shocks in one segment with growth in the other, though the upcoming DePuy Synthes spinoff signals J&J itself sees value in *narrowing* focus within MedTech even as it keeps the overall two-segment structure.
  - **R&D scale:** $14–17B/year — one of the largest R&D budgets in healthcare, funding a 200+ compound pipeline plus continuous device iteration.
  - **Balance sheet / M&A firepower:** repeated large acquisitions (Abiomed $16.6B, Intra-Cellular $14.6B, Ambrx $2B) show J&J using its scale to buy pipeline and platform technology rather than relying purely on in-house discovery.
- **Barriers to entry / structural pressure:**
  - **Regulatory barriers:** FDA/EMA approval pathways for both drugs and devices are lengthy, expensive, and require large trials — a natural moat against new entrants, but it cuts both ways (multi-year timelines, high failure rates).
  - **Patent cliffs:** Stelara's 2025 biosimilar erosion (above) is the live, current example — steep, fast revenue decline once several biosimilars launch and PBMs switch formulary preference. A recurring structural risk for any big pharma company, actively playing out in J&J's numbers right now.
  - **Device recalls / safety scrutiny:** the ongoing Impella/AIC and Cerepak recalls illustrate that MedTech carries its own version of "regulatory risk" — Class I recalls, cybersecurity vulnerabilities in connected medical devices, and product liability exposure.

**Major litigation overhang — talc/LTL Management bankruptcy saga — now heading toward resolution as of late July 2026:**[^2026-07-29a]

One of the largest and most closely watched mass-tort situations in U.S. corporate history. Thousands of plaintiffs allege J&J's talc-based baby powder (discontinued in the US/Canada in 2020, globally in 2023) contained asbestos and caused ovarian cancer and mesothelioma. Timeline:

- **October 2021:** J&J created a subsidiary, **LTL Management LLC**, and had it file Chapter 11 bankruptcy in North Carolina — a controversial "Texas two-step" style maneuver meant to isolate talc liabilities from the parent company's balance sheet while a settlement was negotiated, pausing all talc lawsuits.
- **January 2023:** the Third Circuit Court of Appeals reversed the bankruptcy court, ruling J&J/LTL wasn't in genuine financial distress and shouldn't be allowed to use bankruptcy this way.
- **April 2023:** the case was dismissed by the NJ bankruptcy court; LTL immediately **re-filed** a second Chapter 11 petition in New Jersey.
- **July 2024:** the Third Circuit again upheld dismissal of the bankruptcy petition.
- J&J then pursued a **third** attempt, planning to file in Texas under a renamed entity (**Red River Talc**), claiming it had secured sufficient claimant votes in a revised reorganization plan.
- **March 31, 2025:** a Texas bankruptcy judge **rejected** this third attempt/plan. J&J said it would not appeal.
- **April–June 2025:** with the bankruptcy route closed off (for now), the underlying talc multidistrict litigation (MDL) resumed active case management; a status conference was set for June 17, 2025.
- **June 2026:** J&J **won** its most recent individual talc ovarian-cancer jury trial in California — an example of J&J notching some trial wins even as the broader mass-tort exposure remained unresolved.
- **July 27–28, 2026 — landmark $5.5 billion global settlement announced:** J&J agreed to pay an estimated **$5.5 billion** to resolve **~76,000 ovarian-cancer talc claims** (covering the federal MDL in New Jersey plus related state-court cases) — effectively all remaining talc claims.
  - Requires **95% acceptance** among ovarian-cancer claimants in state and federal courts to become final.
  - Payment schedule: ~$3B in 2027, remainder in 2028, with all accepted claims paid out within 18 months (versus a decade-plus under prior proposals).
  - Covers **only existing claims, not future lawsuits** — excluding future claims freed up more money for current plaintiffs and sped up payment timing.
  - J&J's VP of Litigation, Erik Haas, said the company still denies the products caused cancer or were unsafe, framing this as resolving liability rather than admitting fault.
- **Net takeaway:** after three failed bankruptcy attempts (2021, 2023, 2025), J&J pivoted to a direct global settlement — as of this writing the most credible path yet to closing out the ovarian-cancer talc overhang, though **not yet final** (pending the 95% opt-in threshold) and it does not address future claims or the separate mesothelioma/asbestos claims. A major de-risking event for the stock/balance sheet if it closes, but should be flagged as *pending*, not resolved — reported earnings/EPS above do not yet reflect the settlement's cash impact.[^2026-07-29a] As of this reformatting pass (early August 2026), the settlement's status was still described as pending the 95% acceptance threshold, with roughly 68,900 talc lawsuits still on the docket in New Jersey — consistent with, not a change from, the picture above.[^2026-08-04]
