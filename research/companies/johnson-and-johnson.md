# Johnson & Johnson (NYSE: JNJ)

*Last researched: 2026-07-29 (refreshed same-day per updated research process)*

## 1. Company Overview

**Headquarters:** New Brunswick, New Jersey (world headquarters campus is right next to Rutgers University's main campus — the two institutions have deep historical ties in the New Brunswick community).

**Founding story:** Founded in 1886 by three brothers — Robert Wood Johnson I, James Wood Johnson, and Edward Mead Johnson — after Robert Wood Johnson heard a speech by antiseptic-surgery pioneer Joseph Lister in 1885 and was inspired to commercialize sterile, ready-to-use surgical dressings (at the time, most bandages were prepared crudely at the bedside, and infection was a huge cause of surgical death). The company started with 14 employees (8 women, 6 men) and was formally incorporated in 1887, one year after operations began. Robert Wood Johnson I served as first president.

**Current CEO:** Joaquín Duato — Spanish-American executive, born 1962 in Valencia, Spain (dual Spain/US citizen). Holds an MBA from ESADE Business School (Barcelona) and a Master of International Management from Thunderbird School of Global Management (Arizona). Joined J&J in 1989 through Janssen Pharmaceuticals in Spain and rose through the ranks over three decades. Named CEO in August 2021 (effective January 2022), the company's 8th CEO since going public in 1944. He is also Chairman of the Board.

**Corporate structure:** J&J historically ran three segments (Pharmaceutical, MedTech, Consumer Health). In 2023 it **spun off its Consumer Health division as Kenvue** (maker of Tylenol, Band-Aid, Listerine, Neutrogena) via IPO and subsequent split-off — this was one of the largest corporate spinoffs in history. J&J is now a pure-play **biopharma + medical technology** company organized into two segments:
- **Innovative Medicine** (pharmaceuticals) — ~48% of 2025 sales
- **MedTech** (devices) — ~51%/52% of 2025 sales

**A further major structural change is underway**: on October 14, 2025, J&J announced it will **separate its Orthopaedics business** (DePuy Synthes) into a standalone public company within 18–24 months, most likely via a tax-free spinoff. Orthopaedics is about 30% of the MedTech segment (~$9.2B in FY2024 sales) and has been growing more slowly than the rest of the portfolio. Namal Nawana was named Worldwide President of DePuy Synthes to lead it through separation and continue running it afterward. This is a significant, very recent (still-unfolding) event — as of mid-2026 it had not yet closed.

**Scale:**
- 2025 full-year revenue: **$94.2 billion** (6.0% reported growth, 5.3% operational growth)
- Innovative Medicine: $28.8B–$45.7B depending on source cut (segment reporting is inconsistent across secondary sources — verify against the 10-K directly for precise figures); MedTech: ~$30.4B
- Employees: **~138,200** (2025)
- Operates in ~60 countries; sells products in 175+ countries; ~250 subsidiaries
- Market capitalization: **~$599 billion as of July 2026** (was ~$560B+ in early 2026) — one of the largest healthcare companies in the world by market cap
- 2025 adjusted diluted EPS: $10.58 (up 9.7% from $9.64 in 2024); reported EPS $11.03
- **Q2 2026 update:** revenue reached **$25.3 billion** (up 6.6% YoY), beating consensus; Innovative Medicine sales +7.8% to $16.38B, MedTech +4.5% to $8.93B. J&J raised full-year 2026 guidance to $99.9B–$100.6B in sales (operational growth 6.5–7.1%) and adjusted operational EPS to $11.50–$11.65 — putting the company on track to cross **$100 billion in annual revenue for the first time in its 140-year history**.[^2026-07-29b]

**Mission (as stated by the company):** to profoundly change the trajectory of health for humanity, leveraging its dual position in pharma and medtech to address disease across the full spectrum from prevention/diagnosis through treatment.

## 2. Technology & Products / Pipeline

### Innovative Medicine (pharma) segment

Key therapeutic areas: **Oncology, Immunology, Neuroscience, Pulmonary Hypertension, Infectious Diseases, and Cardiovascular & Metabolism.**

**Major marketed drugs:**
- **Darzalex (daratumumab)** — a monoclonal antibody for multiple myeloma (blood cancer), one of J&J's largest and fastest-growing drugs.
- **Stelara (ustekinumab)** — historically J&J's top immunology drug (psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's, ulcerative colitis), but **lost patent exclusivity in 2025 and is now in steep, well-documented decline**: sales fell ~8.1% (operational) in Q1 2025 and an accelerating 42.7% in Q2 2025 as 8+ biosimilars (Amgen's Wezlana, Sandoz's Pyzchiva, Biocon's Yesintek, and others) launched at 46–90% discounts, and PBMs (CVS, Optum) began excluding or de-prioritizing it on formularies. Stelara was ~18% of Innovative Medicine sales in 2024 (peak ~$11B in 2023); analysts project it falling to roughly $2.7B by 2027. This is the textbook "patent cliff" case study for the segment.
- **Tremfya (guselkumab)** — J&J's newer IL-23 inhibitor, positioned as Stelara's successor. Recently expanded: FDA approved a label update (2026) making it the only IL-23 inhibitor shown to inhibit structural joint damage progression in psoriatic arthritis, and it's also approved for Crohn's disease with both subcutaneous and IV induction options — a differentiator versus competitors.
- **Erleada (apalutamide)** — androgen receptor inhibitor for prostate cancer.
- **Rybrevant (amivantamab)** — a **bispecific antibody** for EGFR-mutated non-small-cell lung cancer, often combined with Lazcluze (lazertinib). In December 2025 the FDA approved **Rybrevant FASPRO** (amivantamab + hyaluronidase), the first subcutaneous (under-the-skin) version — turning what was a lengthy IV infusion into a quick injection, a big quality-of-life upgrade for cancer patients; dosing schedule further simplified in Feb 2026.
- **Carvykti (ciltacabtagene autoleucel)** — a **CAR-T cell therapy** for relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, developed with Legend Biotech, FDA-approved for earlier treatment lines in April 2024. It's the first/only BCMA-targeted CAR-T with this label position.
- **Spravato (esketamine)** — nasal spray for treatment-resistant depression, notable as a ketamine-derived psychiatric drug administered under medical supervision.
- **Invega** franchise (paliperidone, various long-acting injectable formulations) — for schizophrenia.

**Recent M&A / pipeline moves:**
- **Intra-Cellular Therapies acquisition (2025)** — J&J's largest recent bio-pharma deal, announced Jan 2025 and closed **April 2, 2025**, for **$132/share cash, ~$14.6 billion total equity value**. Brings **CAPLYTA (lumateperone)**, the only FDA-approved drug for bipolar I/II depression as both monotherapy and adjunct, plus schizophrenia approval and a pipeline into generalized anxiety disorder and Alzheimer's-related psychosis/agitation. Expected to add ~$0.7B incremental sales in 2025 (~0.8pp of growth) but dilute adjusted EPS by ~$0.25 in 2025, narrowing to ~$0.21 in 2026 as financing costs are offset by operational accretion.
- **Ambrx acquisition** — completed March 7, 2024, ~$2.0B all-cash (~$1.8B net of cash), $28.00/share. Brings a synthetic-biology **antibody-drug conjugate (ADC)** platform for next-gen targeted cancer therapies.
- **Abiomed acquisition** — completed December 22, 2022 (an earlier deal, not 2024/2025 as sometimes assumed), ~$16.6B enterprise value at $380/share. This sits in the MedTech segment (cardiovascular), not Innovative Medicine.
- **Correction on Cerevel Therapeutics**: this company was **acquired by AbbVie, not J&J** — AbbVie announced the deal Dec 6, 2023 and closed it Aug 1, 2024 for ~$8.7B ($45/share), for schizophrenia/Parkinson's/mood-disorder pipeline assets. If your source material said "J&J acquired Cerevel," that appears to be a mix-up with a competitor's deal — worth double-checking wherever that claim originated.
- R&D investment: **>$14–17B in 2025** depending on measurement window (trailing-twelve-month R&D spend as of mid-2025 was ~$17.0B, up ~14% YoY), roughly 15–16% of sales — supporting a stated pipeline of 200+ compounds in development. J&J also announced a commitment to invest **>$55 billion in U.S. manufacturing/R&D over the next four years**, a 25% increase versus the prior four-year period.

### MedTech segment

- **Surgery — Ethicon**: sutures, staplers, energy devices, wound closure. In 2025 J&J launched a restructuring of the Surgery franchise to exit non-strategic product lines (~$29M in restructuring costs recorded in fiscal Q2 2025).
- **Orthopaedics — DePuy Synthes**: joint replacement (hips, knees), trauma/spine hardware. Slated for spinoff as a standalone company (see above) — currently ~$9.2B/year in sales, largest comprehensive orthopaedics-focused business by market share once independent.
- **Cardiovascular**: **Abiomed's Impella** heart pumps and **Biosense Webster** electrophysiology (cardiac mapping/ablation catheters for treating arrhythmias like atrial fibrillation).
- **Vision**: **Acuvue** contact lenses and **Tecnis** intraocular lenses (IOLs, implanted during cataract surgery).

**Recent MedTech recall activity (2025–2026) — notable, ongoing:**
- **Automated Impella Controller (AIC)**: a cascading series of recalls tied to a significant **cybersecurity vulnerability**, affecting 100,000+ devices — separate recall actions confirmed in July, September, October (x2), and December 2025.
- **Impella RP / Impella RP with SmartAssist / Impella RP Flex**: Class I recalls over inaccurate differential-pressure sensor readings.
- **Cerepak detachable coil systems** (Cerenovus business, used in brain aneurysm treatment): recalled for a higher-than-expected failure-to-detach rate, linked to 4 serious injuries and 1 death.

This recall cluster is worth flagging as a real quality/reliability signal in the cardiovascular device business, distinct from and in addition to the talc litigation (see Financials).

### Simple explainers of the underlying tech

- **CAR-T cell therapy (Carvykti)**: doctors draw a patient's own T-cells (immune cells) from blood, genetically re-engineer them in a lab to display a receptor (a "CAR," chimeric antigen receptor) that recognizes a specific tag on the cancer cell (here, BCMA, a marker on myeloma cells), multiply the modified T-cells, then infuse them back into the patient. The re-engineered T-cells now hunt and kill any cell displaying that BCMA tag. It's essentially turning a patient's own immune system into a living, targeted drug — powerful but complex and expensive to manufacture per-patient (unlike an off-the-shelf pill).
- **Impella heart pump**: a catheter-based miniature pump, threaded through an artery (often from the leg) up into the heart's left ventricle, that mechanically pushes blood forward to take over some or all of the heart's pumping work. It's used temporarily during high-risk procedures (like angioplasty) or in acute heart failure/cardiogenic shock, buying the heart time to recover or bridging a patient to a longer-term solution — think of it as a temporary mechanical "assist" for a heart that's struggling to pump enough blood on its own.
- **Bispecific antibodies (Rybrevant)**: a normal antibody has two arms that both grab the same target. A bispecific antibody's two arms are engineered to grab two *different* targets simultaneously — e.g., one arm binds a receptor on the tumor cell (EGFR) and drives a signal-blocking effect, while functioning through dual pathway engagement to overcome resistance mechanisms that a single-target drug can't address. It's a way of getting one molecule to do the coordinated work that used to require two separate drugs.
- **Antibody-drug conjugates (ADC, via Ambrx platform)**: an antibody that "homes in" on a marker specific to cancer cells is chemically linked to a potent toxic payload (essentially a targeted chemotherapy warhead). The antibody acts like a delivery truck, and the payload is released mainly once inside the cancer cell — sparing more of the healthy tissue that traditional systemic chemo damages.

## 3. Market & Competition

**Pharma competitors:** Pfizer, Merck, AbbVie, Roche, Novartis, Eli Lilly, Bristol Myers Squibb, Sanofi, GSK — all large-cap diversified pharma companies competing across oncology, immunology, and other therapeutic areas.

**MedTech competitors:** Medtronic, Stryker, Boston Scientific, Abbott — the other large diversified device makers, each with somewhat different segment emphasis (e.g., Medtronic strong in cardiac devices/diabetes, Stryker strong in orthopaedics/surgical, Boston Scientific strong in cardiovascular/endoscopy, Abbott strong in diagnostics/cardiovascular/nutrition).

**What differentiates J&J:**
- **Dual pharma + medtech model** is genuinely unusual at this scale — most peers are pure-play either biopharma (Pfizer, Merck, AbbVie) or pure-play devices (Medtronic, Stryker). This diversification cushions patent-cliff shocks in one segment with growth in the other, though the upcoming DePuy Synthes spinoff signals J&J itself sees value in *narrowing* focus within MedTech even as it keeps the overall two-segment structure.
- **R&D scale**: $14–17B/year gives J&J one of the largest R&D budgets in healthcare, funding a 200+ compound pipeline plus continuous device iteration.
- **Balance sheet / M&A firepower**: repeated large acquisitions (Abiomed $16.6B, Intra-Cellular $14.6B, Ambrx $2B) show J&J using its scale to buy pipeline and platform technology rather than relying purely on in-house discovery.

**Barriers to entry / structural pressure:**
- **Regulatory barriers**: FDA/EMA approval pathways for both drugs and devices are lengthy, expensive, and require large trials — a natural moat against new entrants, but it cuts both ways (multi-year timelines, high failure rates).
- **Patent cliffs**: Stelara's 2025 biosimilar erosion (see above) is the live, current example of what happens when exclusivity ends — steep, fast revenue decline once several biosimilars launch and PBMs switch formulary preference. This is a recurring structural risk for any big pharma company and is actively playing out in J&J's numbers right now.
- **Device recalls / safety scrutiny**: the ongoing Impella/AIC and Cerepak recalls illustrate that MedTech carries its own version of "regulatory risk" — Class I recalls, cybersecurity vulnerabilities in connected medical devices, and product liability exposure.

**Major litigation overhang — talc/LTL Management bankruptcy saga — now heading toward resolution as of late July 2026:**[^2026-07-29a]
This is one of the largest and most closely watched mass-tort situations in U.S. corporate history. Timeline:
- Thousands of plaintiffs allege J&J's talc-based baby powder (discontinued in the US/Canada in 2020, globally in 2023) contained asbestos and caused ovarian cancer and mesothelioma.
- **October 2021**: J&J created a subsidiary, **LTL Management LLC**, and had it file Chapter 11 bankruptcy in North Carolina — a controversial "Texas two-step" style maneuver meant to isolate talc liabilities from the parent company's balance sheet while a settlement was negotiated, pausing all talc lawsuits.
- **January 2023**: the Third Circuit Court of Appeals reversed the bankruptcy court, essentially ruling J&J/LTL wasn't in genuine financial distress and shouldn't be allowed to use bankruptcy this way.
- **April 2023**: the case was dismissed by the NJ bankruptcy court; LTL immediately **re-filed** a second Chapter 11 petition in New Jersey.
- **July 2024**: the Third Circuit again upheld dismissal of the bankruptcy petition.
- J&J then pursued a **third** attempt, planning to file in Texas under a renamed entity (**Red River Talc**), claiming it had secured sufficient claimant votes in a revised reorganization plan.
- **March 31, 2025**: a Texas bankruptcy judge **rejected** this third attempt/plan. J&J said it would not appeal.
- **April–June 2025**: with the bankruptcy route closed off (for now), the underlying talc multidistrict litigation (MDL) resumed active case management; a status conference was set for June 17, 2025.
- **June 2026**: J&J **won** its most recent individual talc ovarian-cancer jury trial in California — an example of J&J notching some trial wins even as the broader mass-tort exposure remained unresolved at that point.
- **July 27–28, 2026 — landmark $5.5 billion global settlement announced**: J&J agreed to pay an estimated **$5.5 billion** to resolve **~76,000 ovarian-cancer talc claims** (covering the federal MDL in New Jersey plus related state-court cases) — effectively all remaining talc claims. The deal requires **95% acceptance** among ovarian-cancer claimants in state and federal courts to become final. Payment schedule: ~$3B in 2027, remainder in 2028, with all accepted claims to be paid out within 18 months (versus a decade-plus under prior proposals). Notably, the settlement **only covers existing claims, not future lawsuits** — excluding future claims freed up more money for current plaintiffs and sped up payment timing. J&J's VP of Litigation, Erik Haas, said the company still denies the products caused cancer or were unsafe, framing this as resolving liability rather than admitting fault.
- **Net takeaway**: after three failed bankruptcy attempts (2021, 2023, 2025 — see above), J&J pivoted to a direct global settlement, which as of this writing (July 29, 2026) is the most credible path yet to closing out the ovarian-cancer talc overhang — though it is not yet final (pending the 95% opt-in threshold) and does not address any future claims or the separate mesothelioma/asbestos claims. This is a major de-risking event for the stock/balance sheet if it closes, but should still be flagged as *pending*, not resolved, and reported earnings/EPS above do not yet reflect the settlement's cash impact.[^2026-07-29a]
