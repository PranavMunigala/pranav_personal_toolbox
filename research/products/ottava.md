# OTTAVA™ Robotic Surgical System

## What It Does

OTTAVA is Johnson & Johnson MedTech's soft-tissue robotic surgical system — a robot that assists surgeons in performing minimally invasive general surgery procedures (the kind of soft-tissue work done through small incisions in the abdomen, as opposed to orthopedic/bone procedures). It received FDA De Novo market authorization on July 22, 2026, becoming the first real challenger to Intuitive Surgical's da Vinci system in the soft-tissue robotic surgery category after roughly two decades of da Vinci having the space essentially to itself.

The problem it's solving isn't really "can a robot do surgery" — that's established — it's making robotic surgery cheaper and more space-efficient to deploy, so more hospitals (not just large, well-resourced ones) can offer it.

## How It Works

Picture a normal OR table, except four robotic arms are built directly into it rather than mounted on a separate rolling cart or overhead boom (the design almost every competing system, including da Vinci, uses). Think of it like the difference between a kitchen island with a built-in stove versus wheeling a portable cooktop into the room each time — OTTAVA's arms live in the furniture itself and fold away under the table when not needed.

Two features stand out for someone new to the space:
- **Predefined poses**: at the push of a button, motors and software drive the arms into standard procedural setup positions, instead of a surgical tech manually positioning each arm — similar to a car seat "memory" preset moving several motors to a saved position at once.
- **Twin-motion**: table and arms move together as one coordinated unit. In multi-quadrant surgeries (where the surgeon needs access to different parts of the abdomen during one procedure), repositioning the patient normally means re-adjusting every arm afterward — twin-motion keeps the arms correctly aligned automatically when the table tilts or shifts, like a phone gimbal keeping a camera stable as your hand moves.

The net effect is a system that takes 30-50% less floor space than boom/cart-mounted competitors, which matters because OR space is often the actual bottleneck to adding robotic capacity, not surgeon skill or willingness.

## Company & Competing Products

Made by **Johnson & Johnson MedTech** (a division of Johnson & Johnson). No company-level profile exists yet in `research/companies/` — worth doing a separate J&J MedTech company research pass if you want the fuller picture (financials, leadership, etc.); this file is scoped to the product itself.

Competing systems in the soft-tissue robotic surgery space:
- **Intuitive Surgical — da Vinci**: the incumbent, ~71% market share, 9,200+ installed systems, unchallenged in this category for ~20 years until OTTAVA's clearance.
- **Medtronic — Hugo RAS**: the other major recent challenger; modular/portable arm design, priced around $1.5-2M, leverages Medtronic's existing presence in ~98% of US hospitals.
- **CMR Surgical — Versius**: portable, modular, lower capital cost, per-procedure leasing model; more traction in UK/Europe/Asia than the US so far.
- Smaller/earlier-stage entrants: Asensus Surgical (Senhance), Vicarious Surgical, Virtual Incision (MIRA).

Note: J&J also makes **VELYS**, a separate robotic-assisted system focused on orthopedic (joint replacement) surgery — different category from OTTAVA's soft-tissue focus, don't conflate the two.

## Stage & Validation

**Market stage**: Just cleared — FDA De Novo authorization granted July 22, 2026, covering ten general surgery procedure types (Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, gastrectomy, cholecystectomy, splenectomy, gastric sleeve, small bowel resection, appendectomy, lysis of adhesions, fundoplication, hiatal hernia repair). J&J plans an initial limited launch to "select customers" rather than a broad rollout.

**Clinical evidence**: Backed by the FORTE study — a prospective, single-arm, open-label trial across six U.S. hospital sites, 30 patients undergoing Roux-en-Y gastric bypass. All primary safety/performance endpoints were met through 30 days post-procedure, with no conversions to non-robotic (open) surgery. That's a small trial by device-approval standards — reasonable for a De Novo pathway given da Vinci's long safety track record already established the underlying surgical approach, but worth noting the sample size when weighing how "proven" OTTAVA is versus da Vinci's decades of data.

**Regulatory path**: IDE (investigational device exemption) granted November 2024 → FDA De Novo submission January 2026 → authorization July 2026. A U.S. clinical trial for inguinal hernia repair is ongoing, suggesting J&J will keep expanding the approved procedure list over time.

## Personal Takeaways

- Genuinely interesting inflection point: this is the first time in ~20 years a company has cracked da Vinci's soft-tissue monopoly with FDA clearance in hand, not just a prototype demo — worth watching how fast hospital adoption actually moves given switching costs (surgeon retraining, existing da Vinci capital investment at most hospitals).
- The table-integrated design is a genuinely different bet than Medtronic/CMR's "make it portable/modular" approach — worth watching which philosophy wins on cost-per-OR and surgeon preference over the next couple years.
- Small initial trial (30 patients, one procedure type) means real-world safety data over the next 1-2 years of limited launch will matter a lot before drawing conclusions on how it holds up against da Vinci at scale.
- Milestone to watch: results/uptake from the "select customers" limited launch, and whether the inguinal hernia trial leads to an expanded indication.
